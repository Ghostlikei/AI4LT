Two routes to generalization
Can a property of the rule itself certify generalization?
Class-level route
Show 𝐿𝒟︀(ℎ)≈ 𝐿𝑆(ℎ) for many hypotheses 
at once.
Then any 𝖤𝖱𝖬 over the class learns.
Tools: VC, Rademacher, fat-shattering.
Rule-level route
Show the returned predictor barely changes 
when one training point changes.
Then 𝐿𝑆(𝐴(𝑆)) estimates 𝐿𝒟︀(𝐴(𝑆)).
This property of the rule is called stability.
A stable learning rule generalizes, and regularization is what makes a rule stable.

Stability implies generalization
Theorem
If 𝐴 is 𝛽(𝑛)-stable, then for every distribution 𝒟︀,

Convex Lipschitz bounded problems
The warm-up used three facts about absolute-loss linear prediction:
• 𝐿𝑆(𝑤) is convex in 𝑤;
• small movement in 𝑤 gives small change in each loss;
• we compare against predictors of bounded size.
We isolate these facts so the same stability argument applies beyond Euclidean absolute loss.
A convex Lipschitz bounded problem has:
• Convexity: ℓ(𝑤,𝑧) convex in 𝑤;
• Lipschitzness: |ℓ(𝑤,𝑧)− ℓ(𝑤′,𝑧)| ≤ 𝐺‖𝑤− 𝑤′‖;
• Bounded comparison: comparator set such as ‖𝑤‖ ≤ 𝐵 or Ψ(𝑤)≤ 𝐵2.
