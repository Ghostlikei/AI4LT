Why binary VC theory is not enough
Most modern learning is real-valued:
• regression: predict real targets (prices, ratings, scores);
• scale-sensitive: bound generalization by ‖𝑤‖ and margin 𝛾, not just 𝗌𝗂𝗀𝗇(𝑓(𝑥));
• convex surrogate losses: hinge, logistic, squared (week 5).
Where behaviors live on the sample:
• Binary: {−1,+1}𝑛. The growth function counts them (at most 2𝑛).
• Real-valued: ℝ𝑛. Uncountably many. Counting fails.

Pseudo-dimension: VC for real-valued classes
How to extend VC dimension? For binary classes, shatter = realize every ±1 pattern. For real-valued 
ℱ︀⊂ ℝ𝒵︀, the values are continuous, so what does “shatter” mean?
Idea. Place a threshold 𝜃𝑖 at each 𝑧𝑖 and ask: is 𝑓(𝑧𝑖) above or below?
Shattering. ℱ︀ shatters 𝑧1,…,𝑧𝑘 if there exist thresholds 𝜃1,…,𝜃𝑘 ∈ ℝ such that every binary pattern
